The commission came in last September. A Welsh dresser in ash, to fit a specific alcove in a Victorian terraced house in Leeds. Dimensions fixed, but the style left to us. Six months later, it's installed and the client cried when she saw it. Worth every late night.

Here's what I learned.

## Month 1: Design and drawings

I always start with pencil drawings, not CAD. CAD comes later for checking dimensions — but at the start I need to understand the proportions by hand. A Welsh dresser has very specific proportions: the rack (top) at roughly two-thirds the height of the base, the shelves set at intervals that feel generous without being wasteful.

We went through eleven sketch iterations before settling on the final design. The client wanted plate grooves in the shelves (traditional) but a slightly cleaner door profile than most heritage designs. We ended up with a simple flat-panel door in a mortised frame, no moulding.

## Months 2–3: The carcass

Ash is a joy to work. Springy, with a pronounced open grain that takes oil beautifully. The boards came from a sawmill in Cumbria — locally felled, kiln-dried.

The base is frame-and-panel construction throughout. The back panels are floating in grooves cut with the router plane — this allows seasonal movement without splitting.

**What went wrong:** I underestimated the movement in the wide top panel. First test-fit, it had cupped about 3mm across its 600mm width. Back to the bench, re-flatten, adjust the fitting. A day lost, but a useful reminder.

## Month 4: The rack

The rack (upper shelving section) was straightforward by comparison. The main challenge was the cornice moulding — a traditional Welsh dresser has a stepped cornice at the top that catches the eye and anchors the whole piece visually. I cut this with a combination plane, working in multiple passes.

> Getting the cornice right took longer than any other single element. It's ten percent of the surface area and fifty percent of the visual impression.

## Month 5: Finishing

Three coats of Danish oil, cut back between each coat with 000 steel wool. The ash went from pale cream to a warm honey gold. The open grain really drinks in the oil.

Hardware sourced from a traditional ironmonger in the Dales — hand-forged hinges and H-pulls in blackened steel. Period-appropriate but not fussy.

## Month 6: Installation

This is always the nervous part. Walls are never truly plumb. Floors are never truly level. We budget a day for installation and usually need it.

The dresser went in on a Tuesday. It took until Thursday afternoon to get it truly level, secured to the wall properly, and the doors hanging without binding.

## Would I do it again?

Without hesitation. These are the jobs that matter — pieces that will be in families for generations. The client's grandmother had a Welsh dresser she lost in a house move. This one was partly about that.

That's why I do this work.
