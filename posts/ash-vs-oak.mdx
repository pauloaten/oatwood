These are the two timbers we get asked about most. Both are hardwoods. Both are widely available from sustainable British sources. Both make excellent furniture. But they're genuinely different in feel, workability, and the kind of piece they suit best.

## The short version

**Choose ash** if you want a lighter, springier feel with dramatic grain, lower cost, and a more contemporary look.

**Choose oak** if you want maximum hardness, the classic English furniture aesthetic, and a timber that only improves with age.

## Hardness

Oak is harder. On the Janka scale, European oak sits around 1,120 lbf; English ash is around 1,320 lbf, which actually makes ash *harder* — but in practice, both are more than adequate for furniture. The difference you'd notice is dent resistance on a dining table: oak is marginally more forgiving if someone drops a heavy pot.

## Grain and figure

This is where they differ most visually.

**Oak** has a tight, even grain with distinctive medullary rays — those silvery flecks you see on quarter-sawn oak. It's a classic look that reads as traditional, serious, enduring.

**Ash** has a much more dramatic, open grain. The growth rings are wide and strongly figured. In a wide board you get almost flame-like patterns. It's striking in a more obviously modern way.

Neither is better. It depends entirely on the room it's going into and the aesthetic you're after.

## Workability

Both plane and chisel well, but they behave differently at the bench.

Oak is harder on tools — you'll be sharpening more often. It also contains tannins that react with iron, so you'll get black staining if you use uncoated steel fittings or leave steel wool in contact with the surface. Use brass or stainless for screws and hardware.

Ash is slightly more forgiving to work. The open grain can tear out if you go against it, but with the grain it planes like a dream. Bends beautifully with steam — the traditional wood for Windsor chair backs and cricket bats for exactly this reason.

## Cost

Ash is typically 20–30% cheaper than oak for comparable grade timber. This varies by supplier and season, but as a rule of thumb, if budget is a consideration, ash is the sensible choice without compromising quality.

## Our recommendation

For dining tables where you want the classic English furniture look: **oak**.

For shelving, bookcases, or pieces where you want the grain to be the star: **ash**.

For anything with curved or steam-bent elements: **ash**, without question.

For a bedroom piece that will last fifty years and look better at the end of it: either, honestly. Both age beautifully with oil.
